Equations with Fractions

Fractions look like the hard part. One move makes them vanish entirely.

Fractions in an equation look worse than they are. There is one move that makes them disappear completely, and once they're gone you're back to Lesson 0001 territory.

Multiply every term on both sides by the LCD — the least common denominator of all the fractions in the equation. Every denominator divides out, and you're left with an ordinary equation.

The word that does all the work there is every. The whole-number terms get multiplied too. That is the entire lesson, and it's the one thing people forget.

Worked example 1 — the LCD hits every term
x3 + 12 = 56Denominators are 3, 2 and 6. The LCD is 6.
6·x3 + 6·12 = 6·56Multiply each term by 6 — all three of them.
2x + 3 = 5Every denominator divides out. No fractions left.
2x = 2Subtract 3 from both sides.
x = 1Divide by 2.

Check: 13 + 12 = 26 + 36 = 56. ✓

The mistake to inoculate yourself against

Look at x5 − 1 = 2. Multiplying by 5 has to hit the −1 and the 2 as well, not just the fraction:

x − 5 = 10, so x = 15  ✓
x − 1 = 10, so x = 11  ✗  (the −1 never got multiplied)

It's the same failure as forgetting to distribute in Lesson 0002: an operation that has to reach everything only reached part of it.

Worked example 2 — a fraction on each side
x + 14 = x − 32LCD of 4 and 2 is 4.
x + 1 = 2(x − 3)Multiply both sides by 4. The right side had a 2 on the bottom, so 4/2 = 2 is left over.
x + 1 = 2x − 6Distribute — the parentheses matter here.
x = 7Subtract x, add 6.

Check: 84 = 2 and 42 = 2. ✓

Finding the LCD fast

Run through the multiples of the largest denominator and stop at the first one the others all divide into. For 3, 4 and 6: try 6 (3✓ 4✗), try 12 (3✓ 4✓ 6✓) — the LCD is 12.

Multiplying all the denominators together always works too. It just leaves bigger numbers to simplify later.

Write it like the rubric is watching

  • Show the multiplication step. Write the line where every term gets multiplied by the LCD. That line is the proof you knew what to do — skip it and a grader can't tell whether you understood or guessed.
  • State the LCD. One short phrase — "multiply both sides by 12" — is exactly the kind of justification the syllabus asks for.
  • Check by substituting back into the original equation, fractions and all.
