The five units, in order

This is the whole year. Everything you learn is a tool that belongs to one of these five boxes — when a problem looks unfamiliar, the first move is figuring out which box it came from.

  1. Expressions and Equations Writing and solving equations with precision. Variables on both sides, the distributive property, fractions, solving for a variable. Everything later in the year sits on top of this.
  2. Inequalities Like equations, but the answer is a range instead of a number — and one rule (multiplying or dividing by a negative) flips the sign.
  3. Relations, Functions & Linear Equations Graphs, tables, slope, and functions. Where algebra becomes visual and you start seeing the same relationship four different ways.
  4. Systems of Equations Two equations, two unknowns, one answer that satisfies both. Substitution, elimination, graphing.
  5. Exponents, Polynomials & Factoring Multiplying and breaking apart expressions. Factoring is the gateway to quadratics and to every math class after this one.

The rules that actually affect your grade

The relearning policy is real insurance — use it, don't plan on it

If a checkpoint comes back below 80%, you can replace that score. The reassessed grade replaces the original in Blackbaud. But it has conditions, and they're strict:

  • Conference with your teacher about what went wrong
  • Attend extra help if needed
  • Correct or complete the original task
  • Complete additional learning activities
  • Within 1 week of the original checkpoint, and before the unit summative assessment

Two things follow. First: the week deadline means you act the day you get the score back, not the day before the summative. Second: relearning only covers checkpoints — it will not save a summative.

“Missing” is a zero, and it can become permanent

The gradebook indicators are not all equal:

IndicatorEffect on grade
MissingTreated as a zero
LateNone — but days late are recorded
IncompleteNone; graded as-is against the rubric
Collected / ExemptNone

Notice that Late costs you nothing on the grade but Missing is a zero. If something isn't done, turning it in late still beats not turning it in.

And the escape hatch closes: a missing assignment turned in before the summative gets replaced with its real score. After the summative, the zero is permanent.

The AI policy — read this one carefully

School policy, quoted in the syllabus: under no circumstances may you submit work generated by an AI program as your own. The syllabus specifically names “entering a prompt into an artificial intelligence tool and using the output” as plagiarism — a Level II offense, which can make a student ineligible for honor societies, school trips, and leadership positions.

AI is permitted only when a teacher instructs you to use it, and anything it generates must be cited like any other source.

What that means for this workspace: these lessons are study material, the same category as a textbook or a Khan Academy video. Reading them, practicing with them, and being quizzed by them is studying. Copying anything from them onto an assignment is not. When you want help with an assigned problem, ask your tutor for a similar problem with different numbers — learn the method, then do the real one yourself. If you're ever unsure, the syllabus says to ask your teacher. That's the right move.

How a top student plays this year

  • Check Blackbaud daily. The syllabus asks for it twice — for assignments and for verifying your own grades are entered correctly. Catching a wrongly-entered score is free points.
  • Go to extra help before you need it. Monday and Thursday. Showing up with a specific question while you're doing fine is the single biggest difference between good students and top ones.
  • Write like the rubric is watching — because it is. The course goal names “precision in mathematical communication” and being able to “justify solutions.” Show every step. Say why.
  • Study with people, but never share work. The honor code counts “unauthorized collaboration” as dishonesty. Explaining a concept to a classmate is legitimate and is also the best way to learn it. Handing them your paper is not. If in doubt, ask.
  • Tell your teacher yourself if you miss a test. That's on the student, per the syllabus — and you can't do extracurriculars until a missed assessment is made up.
